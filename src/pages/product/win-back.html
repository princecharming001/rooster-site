How it works

Consent first, then the message

Consent gets captured at the job

Rooster gives you a one-line checkbox for your invoice, booking page or checkout. No checkbox, no text. Email still works.

Rooster picks this week's list

Who's due, who went quiet, who has an open quote. Each name comes with the reason and the draft.

You approve, replies come to you

Messages send inside quiet hours, three a day max per customer, STOP always honored. Replies land in your feed or your phone.

Guardrails

Texting rules, built in

Texting laws changed in 2025 and the fines are real. Rooster treats every message as marketing and follows the strict version of the rules.

  • Written consent recorded before any text, tied to the customer's number
  • Sends only 8am to 8pm in the customer's local time, tighter than federal rules
  • STOP, QUIT, or anything like it stops every campaign for that customer instantly
  • Registered sender identity for your business, so carriers don't filter you as spam

Questions

Is this legal?

Yes when done right, which is why consent, quiet hours and opt-outs are enforced by the software rather than left to memory. Rooster follows the strict reading of federal and state texting law. We recommend you also confirm your own consent language with a lawyer.

What if I don't have customer phone numbers?

Rooster starts with email and with the numbers you collect going forward. Most businesses build a usable list within a couple of months.

Does it work with my booking or job software?

Rooster reads job history from tools like Jobber, Housecall Pro, Square and Booksy where the integration exists, and from a spreadsheet export where it doesn't.
