The fifteen-minute morning

Most small business marketing happens in bursts. A Sunday night of posting, a January push for reviews, a summer where nothing gets done because summer. Bursts feel productive. They don't work, because the things that bring customers back are all timing problems, and timing needs a daily rhythm.

Why daily beats monthly

A review request is worth the most the morning after the job. A reply to a bad review is worth the most the same day. A reminder that the furnace is due is worth the most in the week before the first cold night, not the month after. None of this can be batched. It has to happen when it happens.

The owners who get this right don't have more time. They have a small habit: a fixed slot every morning, short enough to survive a busy day.

What goes in the fifteen minutes

  • Reviews (3 minutes). Reply to anything new. Ask yesterday's customers.
  • Follow-ups (5 minutes). Quotes that went quiet, customers who are due, anyone who said "later."
  • One post (4 minutes). A photo from yesterday's work. One sentence about what it was. Post it to Google as well as Instagram.
  • One check (3 minutes). Are your hours right on Google? Is the special on the menu still the special?

That's it. Not a content calendar. Not a strategy. Four small things, every day, in the same order.

How to make it stick

Attach it to something you already do: the first coffee, the drive to the first job (passenger seat only), the ten minutes before you unlock the door. Keep a list of yesterday's customers where you can see it. Lower the bar until it's embarrassing: one post, not three; one reply, not all of them.

We built Rooster to do the reading, the drafting and the remembering, so the fifteen minutes is just the deciding. But the routine works without us too. The point is the rhythm.
